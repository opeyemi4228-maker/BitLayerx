We turn down app projects fairly often. Usually because the client will get everything they want from a good website, for a fraction of the money.

## The test

Answer honestly.

**Will the same person use it more than once a week?** If not, you do not need an app. Nobody keeps an app they open twice a year.

**Do you need something only a phone can do?** Camera, location, notifications, working with no signal. If none of those matter, a website does the job.

**Can people find you by searching?** Websites appear on Google. Apps do not. If new customers find you by searching, an app cannot replace your site.

**Will people install it?** This is the hard one. Getting somebody to install an app is far harder than getting them to open a link. You need a strong reason.

If you answered no to most of these, build a very good website instead and spend the difference on getting people to it.

## When an app is right

- Staff using it every day for their work
- Customers ordering repeatedly, like food or transport
- Anything needing notifications to work
- Anything needing to work with no signal
- Anything where the camera or location is central

## The middle option most people miss

A modern website can be saved to a phone's home screen, send notifications and work offline. It opens like an app, but people find it on Google and nobody has to install anything from a store.

For a lot of businesses this is the right answer, and it costs far less than two native apps.
