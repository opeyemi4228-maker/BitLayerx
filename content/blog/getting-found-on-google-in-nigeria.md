Most Nigerian businesses rank for their own company name and nothing else. That is the same as not ranking at all, because the people already searching your name are already your customers.

The money is in the searches from people who do not know you exist yet.

## Start with the searches that mean someone is buying

There is a difference between these two searches:

- *"what is interior design"*
- *"interior designer in lekki prices"*

The first is somebody reading. The second is somebody about to spend money. Almost every business optimises for the first kind because the traffic numbers are bigger, then wonders why the traffic never turns into anything.

The shape of a buying search is usually one of these:

- A service plus a place. *"accountant in ikeja"*
- A service plus a price. *"cost of solar installation lagos"*
- A problem stated plainly. *"my website is not showing on google"*
- A comparison. *"paystack vs flutterwave fees"*

Write the page that answers one of those completely, and you will outrank a much larger competitor whose page answers it partially.

## Google Business Profile is the highest return hour you will spend

For any business a customer could physically visit or call, this outranks almost everything else you could do, and it is free.

Claim the profile, then:

- Get the category exactly right, because it is the strongest single signal
- Put the real opening hours in, and keep them true
- Add photographs of the actual premises, not stock images
- Ask every satisfied customer for a review, every time, forever

Reviews are the part people skip because asking feels awkward. It is also the part that decides who appears in the map results. A business with forty reviews beats a better business with four, and that is simply how the ranking works.

## Say the place in the words on the page

Google will not rank you for Lagos because you are in Lagos. It ranks you for Lagos because the page says Lagos, in a sentence a human would actually write.

That does not mean stuffing the word in thirty times. It means the page mentions the areas you serve, the landmarks people use, and the way customers actually describe where they are. A page that says "we deliver across Lekki, Ajah and Victoria Island, usually same day" is doing more for local search than one that says "nationwide coverage".

If you serve several cities, give each one its own page with genuinely different content. One page that lists twenty cities ranks for none of them.

## Speed is a ranking factor here more than most places

Most of your traffic is a phone on mobile data, and Google measures the experience of that phone.

A site that takes eight seconds to load loses most of its visitors before they see anything, and gets ranked lower for the privilege. We wrote separately about [why sites are slow and how to find the cause](/blog/why-your-website-is-slow), but the short version is compress your images, remove the plugins nobody uses, and stop sending an empty page that JavaScript has to fill in.

## Publish the thing your competitors will not

The single most effective page we have ever published is the one about [what a website actually costs in Nigeria](/blog/what-a-website-costs-in-nigeria), for one reason: almost nobody in this market will put prices in writing.

Every industry here has a question like that. The price. The real timeline. What goes wrong. What you should not buy. Whoever answers it first, honestly and in public, takes the search result and the credibility that comes with it.

This is not a clever trick. It works because it is genuinely useful and because your competitors are too nervous to copy it.

## What to ignore

- **Buying backlinks.** It is the fastest way to get a penalty
- **Keyword density.** Not a thing, and has not been for years
- **Blogging weekly with nothing to say.** Twelve thin posts lose to one that answers a real question completely
- **Meta keywords.** Google has ignored this tag for over a decade
- **Anybody guaranteeing position one.** Nobody can guarantee that, and the ones who claim it are usually buying the links in the first point

## The order to do it in

1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. One hour
2. Fix load speed. One day, and it helps rankings and conversions at once
3. Write one genuinely complete page for each service plus place you sell. One week
4. Ask every customer for a review. Forever
5. Publish the thing your competitors will not. Monthly

Then wait. Search results move on a scale of weeks and months, not days, and the single most common mistake after doing the work is undoing it because nothing happened in a fortnight.
