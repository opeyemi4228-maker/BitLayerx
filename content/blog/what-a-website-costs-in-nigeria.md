Ask five agencies in Abuja what a website costs and you will get five refusals to answer until you book a call. That is a sales tactic, not a pricing model. Here is what the work actually costs, and why.

## The short version

| What you are buying | Realistic range (₦) | Realistic range (£) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-page site, template-based | 250,000 - 600,000 | 400 - 950 |
| Marketing site, 5-8 pages, custom design | 1.2m - 3.5m | 1,900 - 5,500 |
| Site plus CMS your team actually runs | 2.5m - 6m | 4,000 - 9,500 |
| Web application with accounts and payments | 8m - 40m+ | 12,000 - 60,000+ |

Those are honest mid-market numbers for 2026. You can pay less. You will usually pay for it twice.

## The three questions that move the number

### 1. Who writes the words?

Copy is the single most underestimated line item on every project. A five-page site needs roughly 2,500 words that have to be *decided*, not just typed  -  what you claim, in what order, with what proof.

If you supply finished copy, you save real money. If you supply a logo and a vague sense of "make it professional", someone bills you to make those decisions, and it is usually the most expensive person in the room.

### 2. Does it need to do anything, or does it need to say something?

A site that says something is a design and content problem. A site that *does* something  -  accounts, payments, dashboards, uploads  -  is a software problem, and software problems carry testing, security, and a support burden that never ends.

The jump from "brochure" to "application" is where budgets multiply. Be honest about which one you are commissioning, because a brochure quote for an application is the most common way projects die.

### 3. What happens after launch?

Most quotes end at launch. Most costs do not. Hosting, a domain, SSL, backups, dependency updates, the small changes you will want in month two  -  none of it is free, and an agency that never mentions it is either hiding it or has not thought about it.

Budget 15-25% of build cost per year for a site that stays healthy. A site nobody maintains is a security incident with a countdown on it.

## Why cheap sites cost more

The 150,000-naira site is real and you can buy one today. What you typically get:

- A page builder your developer knows and you do not
- Assets that were never optimised, so it loads in eight seconds on Nigerian mobile data
- No analytics, so you cannot tell whether it works
- No structured data, so Google struggles to understand what you do
- A developer who stops answering in month three

The rebuild then costs full price, plus whatever the year of poor performance cost you in customers you never heard from.

## What you should ask for in writing

Before you sign anything, ask for these. An agency that will not put them in writing is telling you something.

1. **Who owns the code and the accounts.** You should. Not the agency, not "the developer".
2. **Page load target on 3G.** Under three seconds is achievable. Ask them to commit.
3. **What happens if you leave.** Can the next team pick it up, or is it locked in a proprietary builder?
4. **The maintenance number.** Annual, in writing, before launch.
